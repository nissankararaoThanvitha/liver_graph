Abstract6
Background:Fibrosis stage is the strongest histological predictor of outcome in metabolic dysfunction-7
associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), yet the hepatic transcriptional changes across stages F0–F4,8
and their reproducibility across cohorts, remain incompletely characterised because most studies compare9
broad disease categories or pool stages.10
Methods:We integrated eight public human liver RNA-sequencing datasets comprising 1,085 samples11
and 1,027 recorded patient identifiers and linked them to the knowledge graph. Within-study Spearman12
correlations with fibrosis stage (610 patients) and disease group (216 patients) were combined across13
studieswithadirection-consistencyrequirement. Fibrosis-associatedgeneswereclusteredbyF0–F4profile,14
adjacentstageswerecomparedwithstudy-adjustedmodels,andgeneswereprioritisedwithamulti-evidence15
score tested by leave-one-study-out (LOSO) analysis.16
Results:We identified 3,845 fibrosis-associated genes, 1,822 shared with disease-group associations17
(ρ= 0.702 ). Thesefollowedfivestage-dependentpatterns: elevationfromF1increasingwithstage(fibrillar18
collagens,PDGFRB,TGFB1); elevation from F1 without further increase (proliferation markers); an19
increaselargest between F3andF4 (basement-membraneremodelling, ductular reaction); adecrease largest20
between F1 and F2; and a decrease largest between F3 and F4 (bile-acid, fatty-acid and drug metabolism).21
At equal sample size, only the F3–F4 transition showed many substantial differences, although F4 included22
only 34 patients. Prioritisation identified 447 Tier 1 genes (442 retained in every LOSO refit) and a 23-gene23
shortlist includingPDGFRA,TREM2andCACNA1C.24
Conclusions:Liver fibrosis severity is associated with distinct stage-dependent transcriptional programmes25
rather than a single monotonic response. The resource provides prioritised, stage-annotated candidates for26
cell-resolved and functional validation.27
Keywords:Liver fibrosis; MASLD; Transcriptomics; Meta-analysis; Gene expression patterns; Knowledge28
graph; Gene prioritisation; Fibrosis stage29

Liver transcriptomics has repeatedly linked gene expression to histological severity. Early microarray work40

several studies have integrated public data: meta-analyses of NAFLD and MASLD progression [15,16],46
comparisons of fibrotic and non-fibrotic liver across aetiologies [17], and a multi-cohort analysis of about47
1,000 patients identifying 846 genes that differ between advanced (F3–F4) and mild (F0–F1) fibrosis [18].48
These studies establish that reproducible fibrosis-associated genes exist, but they summarise progression49
as a contrast between categories (Table 1). Pooled designs cannot show whether a gene already differs50
at mild stages, at intermediate stages or only between F3 and F4, although this stage pattern determines51
whether a gene is a plausible early biomarker, a marker of advanced disease or a target whose relevance52
depends on stage. Resolving it requires several methodological safeguards: harmonising expression units,53
gene identifiers and clinical labels across cohorts; handling repeated biopsies so that some patients do not54
carry extra weight; correcting for unequal stage sizes; and testing whether derived patterns are robust to the55
choices used to obtain them [19, 20, 21]. Biomedical knowledge graphs provide a complementary layer for56
interpreting the resulting genes by linking them to pathways, diseases and drugs [22,23,24,25], but are57
rarely combined with patient-level, stage-resolved expression evidence.58

An integrated, patient-level resource of eight liver transcriptomic cohorts212
The integrated resource comprised 1,085 samples and 1,027 recorded patient identifiers (Table 2). Five213
studies provided fibrosis stage for 668 biopsies, which yielded 610 patient-level observations after repeat-214
biopsy handling (F0–F4: 152, 160, 152, 112 and 34 patients), and three studies provided 216 observations215

Fibrosis and disease-group associations largely overlap221
Cross-study testing identified 3,845 genes associated with fibrosis stage (of 14,794 tested) and 4,199222
associated with disease group (of 14,784). The sets shared 1,822 genes and formed a union of 6,222, with223
2,023 fibrosis-only and 2,377 disease-group-only genes (Fig. 2a). Fibrosis-only genes were balanced in224
direction (1,040 rising, 983 falling), whereas disease-group-only genes were predominantly rising (2,042 vs225
335). Among shared genes, the median correlations on the two axes were strongly concordant (Spearman226
ρ= 0.702 , p= 1.9×10 −270): 1,408 genes rose and 384 fell on both axes, and only 30 were discordant227
(falling with fibrosis but rising with disease group; Fig. 2b). Individual associations were moderate, as228
expected for bulk tissue from heterogeneous cohorts (median|ρ|= 0.226 , interquartile range 0.167–0.294;229
890 genes with|ρ| ≥0.30 and 165 with|ρ| ≥0.40 ), so the analysis derives its power from consistency230
across cohorts rather than from large single-gene effects.231

different stages rather than trajectories within individuals; throughout, “early” and “late” refer to lower and239

Discussion330
This study shows that liver fibrosis severity is associated with distinct stage-dependent transcriptional331
programmes rather than a single monotonic expression response. By integrating eight cohorts at the patient332
level and requiring the same direction of association in every study, we identified 3,845 fibrosis-associated333
genes and resolved their F0–F4 profiles into five patterns with different timing, functional content and334
cellular correlates. Three features distinguish these results from earlier grouped-stage analyses: the patterns335
were reproduced by study-adjusted stage models fitted directly to patient-level data; they nested coherently336
within the coarser three-cluster solution; and the transition to cirrhosis was the only step that retained many337
substantial differences at equal sample size, although this transition was also the one represented by the338
fewest patients (34 at F4). Together, they indicate that higher fibrosis stages are associated with a fibrogenic339
matrixprogrammealreadyelevatedatF1,aproliferativesignatureelevatedfromF1withoutfurtherincrease,340
and remodelling and metabolic differences that, in the available data, are most pronounced between F3 and341
F4.342
ThefibrillarmatrixprogrammewasalreadyelevatedatF1andwashigherateachsubsequentstage. Fibrillar343
collagens (COL1A1,COL1A2,COL3A1,COL5A1),PDGFRBandTGFB1belonged to C1, which already344
differed between F0 and F1 and was higher at each subsequent stage up to F4. Lineage-tracing studies345
have identified activated hepatic stellate cells as the dominant source of fibrillar collagen across aetiologies346

A second, distinct programme was elevated from F1 onward but did not increase further at higher stages.355
C3 was enriched for E2F targets and contained proliferation markers (MKI67,TOP2A,CDC45,TYMS)356
together withTREM2. A higher proliferative signal at mild stages is consistent with regenerative and357

longitudinal and cell-resolved studies are needed to test this.364
The C2 programme, the largest cluster and the strongest functional signal in the study, changed most steeply365
between F3 and F4; because F4 was represented by 34 patients, the magnitude and stage location of this366
change should be regarded as provisional. Its Hallmark epithelial–mesenchymal transition enrichment367
should not be read as evidence of hepatocyte EMT, which lineage tracing has not supported [47]. Instead,368
the cluster’s composition—basement-membrane collagens,TIMP1,MMP2, proteoglycans,PDGFRAand369

The falling programmes describe the loss of hepatocyte function. C5, which fell most steeply between F3381

These findings complement previous transcriptomic studies. The matrix and collagen programmes that398
dominated our shared rising genes agree with the fibrosis signatures of Govaereet al.and Hoanget al.399
[11, 10] and with the fibrogenic programmes shared across aetiologies [17]. Comparisons of advanced400

stages; separating them shows that the fibrillar matrix programme already differs at mild stages, whereas402
basement-membrane remodelling, ductular reaction and metabolic loss differ most between F3 and F4.403
The strong concordance between the fibrosis and disease-group axes (1,792 of 1,822 shared genes) further404
indicates that much of the transcriptional response is common to steatohepatitis and fibrosis.405
The framework also has methodological implications. Combining heterogeneous public cohorts without406
a fitted batch-correction model relied on within-study standardisation, rank-based tests that respect the407
ordinal stage scale and a requirement that every testable study agree in direction, an approach that limits the408
influence of study-specific artefacts [21, 19, 20]. Resolving repeat biopsies before stage filtering prevented409
over-weighting of patients with several biopsies, and size-matched refits separated biological from power-410
driven differences between transitions. The cluster analysis illustrates why stability should be reported411
at two levels: average shapes were highly reproducible, but fewer than half of the genes had confident412
assignments, and confident genes were those with stronger stage associations. Reporting membership413
confidence, LOSO retention and preprocessing sensitivity alongside each gene makes the strength of each414
typeofevidenceexplicit, andintegratingtheseresultsinaknowledgegraphkeepseverycandidatetraceable415
from its expression in individual biopsies to its curated pathway, disease and drug relationships [22, 39].416
The stage-resolved view has implications for translational work. Genes that already differ between F0417
and F1 (C1 and C3) are candidate markers of mild fibrosis, whereas C2 and C5 genes, which differ most418
between F3 and F4, are more likely to mark advanced disease. Because individual early differences were419
small at equal sample size, early staging may require multi-gene scores rather than single markers. The420
prioritised genes, each annotated with its pattern, confidence and robustness, provide a ranked starting point421
for such studies, and the 16 shortlisted genes stable across all preprocessing variants are the most defensible422
candidates.423
This study has limitations. First, the cohorts were identified by a targeted rather than a formal systematic424
search, so other eligible datasets may exist. Second, participant overlap across accessions cannot be425
fully excluded from metadata; overlap between two cohorts would make them non-independent, inflating426
combined p-values and directional agreement, although LOSO retention limits the dependence of any result427
on a single cohort. Third, clinical confounding remains possible: age and sex were available for only 327 of428
the 610 staged patients, metabolic and alcohol data were incomplete, which also prevented reclassification429

Conclusions443
Liver fibrosis severity is associated with at least five distinct expression patterns across fibrosis stages: a444
fibrogenic matrix programme elevated from F1 and higher at each later stage, a proliferative signature445
elevated from F1 without further increase, and basement-membrane remodelling, ductular reaction and446
metabolic loss that are most pronounced between F3 and F4, a transition represented by few patients and447
therefore estimated least precisely. The average expression profiles were reproducible across alternative448
clusteringandmodellingconfigurations,althoughindividualgeneassignmentsshowedgreatersensitivityto449
patient resampling. The integrated, uncertainty-aware resource allows each candidate gene to be examined450
together with the evidence for its ranking and its stage assignment. Three priorities follow: testing the451
stage patterns and prioritised genes in independent and longitudinal cohorts with harmonised clinical452
labels and covariates, with larger numbers of patients with cirrhosis; using single-cell or spatial data to453
separate changes in cell state from changes in cell composition, particularly forPDGFRA,TREM2and the454
C2 ductular programme; and public release of the code, graph exports and complete outputs to support455
independent reproduction.456
