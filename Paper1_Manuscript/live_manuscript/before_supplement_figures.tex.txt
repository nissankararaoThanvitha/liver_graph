{\centering\Large\bfseries Stage-Resolved Transcriptomic Analysis Reveals Distinct Molecular Programs Associated with Liver Fibrosis Severity\par}



\section*{Abstract}
Liver fibrosis severity is associated with heterogeneous transcriptional changes whose consistency and biological organisation across cohorts remain incompletely characterised. We integrated eight public human liver transcriptomic datasets comprising 1,085 samples from 1,027 recorded patient identifiers with curated knowledge-graph evidence. Cross-study analyses identified 3,845 fibrosis-associated genes and 4,199 genes associated with ordered disease-group labels, including 1,822 shared genes. Fibrosis-stage profiles across F0--F4 formed five patterns, including early and late increases, an early rise followed by a plateau, and intermediate- and late-stage decreases. Functional enrichment highlighted extracellular-matrix organisation, collagen remodelling, adhesion, immune processes and metabolic programmes. A score combining association magnitude, endpoint separation, adjacent-stage evidence, operational fibrosis-only status and functional annotations identified 447 Tier 1 genes and 23 cluster-representative candidates. Of the Tier 1 genes, 442 retained statistical significance across all five leave-one-study-out refits. Knowledge-graph analysis contextualised prioritised genes through interconnected biological annotations. These findings provide a framework for investigating transcriptional heterogeneity associated with fibrosis severity. The cross-sectional associations and internal robustness assessments require independent longitudinal and functional validation.

\section*{Introduction}
Liver fibrosis is a major determinant of outcomes in fatty liver disease, with advanced stages associated with liver-related complications and mortality \cite{ref1,ref2}. The disease spectrum is now described using metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) terminology \cite{ref3}. Fibrosis may progress, remain stable or regress \cite{singh2015}, motivating investigation of molecular differences associated with its severity.

Hepatic transcriptomic studies have established associations between expression, histological severity and molecular heterogeneity. Hoang et al. identified progressively regulated genes across liver biopsies spanning histological severity \cite{ref4}. Govaere et al. characterised a signature of fibrosing steatohepatitis and evaluated it in an independent cohort \cite{govaere2020}. Cross-cohort analyses have extended this evidence: Piras and DiStefano examined MASLD/MASH signatures through meta-analysis and coexpression analyses \cite{piras2024}, while Yang et al. investigated shared and aetiology-specific fibrogenic programmes \cite{yang2026}. Maiers et al. compared advanced (F3--F4) with mild or absent fibrosis (F0--F1), adding cellular context to the resulting signatures \cite{maiers2026}. These approaches provide complementary perspectives on fibrosis-associated biology.

Biomedical knowledge graphs offer a way to connect expression findings with curated biological relationships. Hetionet demonstrated integration of heterogeneous biomedical resources \cite{himmelstein2017}; OptimusKG extends this approach across molecular, anatomical, clinical and environmental domains \cite{ref5}. Such resources can contextualise candidate genes through pathway and biological-process annotations. Connectivity and annotation sharing, however, do not independently establish causal mechanisms or validate candidates.

Building on these studies, an opportunity remains to combine cross-study directional agreement, gene-expression profiles retaining individual F0--F4 stages, adjacent-stage contrasts and curated annotations within a transparent prioritisation framework. This requires harmonisation of identifiers, expression units and clinical labels, consistent handling of repeated biopsies, and assessment of sensitivity to unequal stage sizes and cohort composition. Gene stage-profile clustering also addresses a different question from patient clustering or coexpression analysis. Because the data are cross-sectional, stage-associated patterns cannot establish molecular progression within individuals.

We aimed to identify reproducible stage-associated transcriptional patterns and characterise their biological organisation. Three questions guided the study: which fibrosis-stage and ordered disease-group associations are directionally consistent across studies; which gene-expression patterns characterise F0--F4; and how statistical and annotation evidence can be combined to prioritise candidates. Eight liver transcriptomic datasets were integrated with OptimusKG and drug data from ChEMBL, DGIdb, GtoPdb and Open Targets; drug annotations were not used for candidate scoring.

The principal contributions were:
\begin{itemize}
\item An integrated resource covering 1,085 samples from eight datasets, with 610 patient-level fibrosis observations and 216 disease-group observations; the graph contained 126,763 nodes and 35,138,047 relationships.
\item Identification of 3,845 fibrosis-associated and 4,199 disease-group-associated genes, including 1,822 shared genes, and five F0--F4 expression patterns.
\item A reproducible scoring framework identifying 447 Tier 1 genes and 23 representative candidates, with 442 Tier 1 genes retaining significance across five leave-one-study-out refits.
\end{itemize}
These contributions concern association, prioritisation and internal robustness; independent validation remains necessary. Results and Discussion present and interpret the findings, followed by Materials and Methods and the Conclusion.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Integrated resource and cross-study gene associations}

Eight human liver transcriptomic datasets comprised 1,085 samples from 1,027 recorded patient identifiers. After repeat-biopsy handling, the fibrosis analysis included 610 patient-level observations across five studies, while the ordered disease-group analysis included 216 observations across three studies. Gene harmonisation retained 53,993 measured genes, including 14,794 shared across all eight datasets.

Integration with OptimusKG and drug data from ChEMBL, DGIdb, GtoPdb and Open Targets produced a resource containing 126,763 nodes and 35,138,047 relationships. Direct queries verified 12,544 Drug nodes and 25,890 drug–gene relationships. Drug annotations were not inputs to the transcriptomic analyses.

Cross-study analysis identified 3,845 fibrosis-associated genes and 4,199 disease-group-associated genes. The sets shared 1,822 genes, with 2,023 selected only in the fibrosis analysis and 2,377 only in the disease-group analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:2}a). Among shared genes, 1,408 showed positive associations on both axes and 384 showed negative associations on both; the remaining 30 showed opposite directions (Fig.~\ref{fig:2}b). These axis-specific categories describe statistical selection across different study populations rather than established biological specificity.

\subsection*{Five expression patterns characterised fibrosis severity}

The fibrosis-associated genes formed five clusters with distinct expression profiles across F0–F4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:3}). C1 contained 857 genes showing an early increase followed by continued elevation. C2 comprised 1,367 genes with a late increase, whereas C3 contained 226 genes showing an early rise followed by a plateau. Decreasing profiles comprised C4, with 397 genes showing a mid-stage decrease, and C5, with 998 genes showing a late decrease.

The five-cluster solution satisfied the predefined gene-subset stability criterion. Mean adjusted Rand index was 0.983 for gene-subset refits, compared with 0.592 for patient-bootstrap refits. Although average patterns were reproducible, individual assignments were less stable: 1,838 genes met the membership-confidence threshold of 0.8. Cluster-number diagnostics are provided in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S1}; individual membership results accompany the analysis outputs. These patterns represent cross-sectional differences between patients at different stages, rather than observed trajectories within individuals.

\subsection*{Adjacent-stage contrasts and enrichment revealed distinct biological contexts}

Study-adjusted comparisons identified 857 substantial expression differences between F0–F1, 1,048 between F1–F2, 1,116 between F2–F3 and 781 between F3–F4, using \(q<0.05\) and \(|\beta|\geq0.2\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:4}a). F2–F3 had the largest number of substantial differences, while F3–F4 had the largest median absolute coefficient, at 0.352 within-study standard-deviation units. However, F3–F4 included only 34 F4 observations. Sample-size-matched refits produced markedly different counts and broad distributions, indicating sensitivity to patient composition and statistical power.

Functional enrichment distinguished immune, matrix-associated and metabolic annotations (Fig.~\ref{fig:4}b). Fibrosis-only increasing genes were enriched for T-cell activation and IL6–JAK–STAT3 signalling, while genes positively associated with both clinical axes were enriched for collagen formation and extracellular matrix organisation. The late-increasing C2 cluster was enriched for the Hallmark epithelial–mesenchymal transition set (\(q=5.25\times10^{-27}\)); C3 was enriched for E2F targets (\(q=1.24\times10^{-14}\)). The late-decreasing C5 cluster was enriched for bile-acid and fatty-acid metabolism.

Enrichment depended partly on cluster-membership confidence, particularly for C4, whose full-cluster and confidence-core analyses shared no significant Reactome term. Complete enrichment results accompany the analysis outputs. These findings indicate annotation over-representation rather than demonstrated activation or suppression of biological processes.

Leave-one-study-out analysis retained statistical significance for 442 of the 447 Tier 1 genes across all five refits, while 247 retained an absolute median correlation of at least 0.30 throughout. All 23 shortlisted genes remained significant, and 18 retained the magnitude threshold. Nevertheless, shortlist membership confidence ranged from 0.38 to 0.99, indicating that high prioritisation scores did not ensure equally stable cluster assignments.

Sixteen candidates were retained across all four label/transformation configurations. Removing the fibrosis-only component while retaining the score threshold reduced the Tier 1 set to 286 genes and the shortlist to 21; this comparison also reduced the maximum achievable score. These analyses assess internal robustness and sensitivity, rather than independent validation.

\subsection*{Knowledge-graph annotations contextualised prioritised genes}

The filtered annotation network connected 42 Tier 1 genes to twelve selected pathway or biological-process annotations through 154 relationships. Enriched annotations included extracellular matrix organisation, cell–cell adhesion, angiogenesis, integrin interactions and PDGF signalling.

Four shortlisted genes appeared in this network: PDGFRA connected to five selected annotations, while EPHA3, NFASC and TREM2 each connected to one. The remaining displayed genes belonged to the wider Tier 1 set and each reached at least three annotations. The network therefore represents annotation sharing within a selected subset, rather than convergence across the complete shortlist. The annotation subgraph is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:5}; complete enrichment statistics accompany the analysis outputs.

\section*{Discussion}
\subsection*{Stage-associated transcriptional heterogeneity}
This study identified distinct transcriptional patterns associated with liver fibrosis severity and organised their statistical and biological context within an integrated knowledge graph. By combining cross-study directional agreement, F0--F4 expression profiles, adjacent-stage contrasts and curated annotations, the framework complements studies linking liver expression to histological severity \cite{ref4}. Its contribution is an interpretable organisation of evidence for candidate investigation; clinical prediction and superiority over existing methods were not evaluated.

Increasing expression patterns were associated with extracellular-matrix, collagen, adhesion and immune annotations, whereas the late-decreasing C5 pattern was enriched for bile-acid and fatty-acid metabolism. These findings suggest differences in tissue-remodelling and metabolic programmes across fibrosis stages. However, enrichment does not establish pathway activation or causal mechanisms, and bulk-liver expression cannot distinguish changes within cells from changes in cellular composition. The shortlisted TREM2 and PDGFRA genes are compatible with independent observations of scar-associated macrophages and mesenchymal populations in human cirrhosis \cite{ref7}, supporting cell-resolved investigation without establishing their cellular origin in these cohorts. Likewise, developmental annotations indicate shared gene membership rather than direct evidence of the named process in liver tissue.

\subsection*{Stability of expression patterns and stage contrasts}
The five-pattern representation was stable under gene subsampling but sensitive to patient composition (mean ARI 0.983 versus 0.592). Only 1,838 genes met the conditional membership-confidence threshold. Because the highest silhouette score occurred at $k=3$, the five clusters represent the stated stability-based resolution rather than a uniquely established biological partition. Individual cluster assignments should therefore be interpreted alongside membership confidence.

Adjacent-stage comparisons provided local expression contrasts, but unequal stage sizes and variable matched-refit results limit claims of a discrete biological transition. F3--F4 had the largest median absolute coefficient, yet included only 34 F4 observations. Study adjustment did not eliminate clinical or cell-composition confounding. Most importantly, these cross-sectional profiles describe differences between patients and cannot establish molecular progression within individuals.

\subsection*{Candidate prioritisation and graph interpretation}
The 23-gene shortlist represents all five patterns without relaxing qualification thresholds for smaller clusters. LOSO significance retention was high among Tier 1 genes, but fewer retained the effect-magnitude criterion across every refit (442 versus 247); the corresponding shortlist counts were 23 and 18. Statistical significance and effect stability therefore provide complementary measures of internal robustness, rather than independent candidate validation. Score components also share evidence, and sensitivity to label, transformation and scoring choices remains relevant. For example, removing the fibrosis-only component reduced Tier 1 membership to 286 genes while retaining the seven-point threshold under a reduced maximum score.

Graph connectivity contextualised a subset of the shortlist: PDGFRA reached five selected annotations, whereas EPHA3, NFASC and TREM2 reached one each. This does not imply convergence of all candidates on the displayed network. Similarly, fibrosis-only status reflects statistical selection in different analysis populations, not established biological specificity. Curated annotation coverage and reuse of annotation evidence in prioritisation constrain interpretation of graph connectivity as corroboration.

\subsection*{Limitations and future validation}
Dataset selection was not systematic, and participant overlap across accessions was unresolved. The 73 retained GSE130970 disease classifications were histology-derived proxies rather than recovered original diagnoses. Incomplete metabolic and alcohol information prevented reconstruction of current MASLD/MASH criteria \cite{ref3}; age and sex were available for only 327 of 610 staged patients. These limitations, unequal stage representation and heterogeneous source measurements restrict generalisability and confounder control.

Input manifests, checksums and explicit parameters support reproducibility, but the retrospectively generated manifest does not document every acquisition decision or establish official HGNC/OptimusKG release dates. The common expression core also does not constitute a complete graph-measurement archive. Drug-annotation coverage varied and therapeutic efficacy was not assessed. Candidate selection was not nested within an independent evaluation design. Independent-cohort replication, longitudinal sampling and functional experiments are therefore needed to establish candidate generalisability, temporal relevance and biological roles.

\section*{Materials and Methods}
\subsection*{Study design and data sources}
We conducted a retrospective computational study of public human liver transcriptomic data to identify fibrosis-severity associations, characterise F0--F4 expression patterns and prioritise candidate genes using curated biological evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:6}). Eight GEO datasets \cite{ref8} comprised 1,085 samples from 1,027 recorded patient identifiers (Table~\ref{tab:1}) \cite{suppli2019,ref4,govaere2020,pantano2021,kozumi2021,ref10,verschuren2024,steinhauser2025}. Identifiers were interpreted within studies; participant independence across accessions was not established.

These human liver tissue datasets provided sample-level expression files and linkable metadata. They were not selected through a documented systematic search. Deposited count, TPM, GCT and per-sample expression files were analysed without new sequencing, alignment or quantification. Clinical annotation availability determined eligibility for each analysis (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S2}).

\subsection*{Clinical annotation and patient handling}
Expression samples were linked to GEO records using study-specific identifiers, titles and descriptions. Harmonised fields included patient identity, biopsy order, fibrosis stage and disease group; original fields and provenance were retained. Fibrosis stages were ordered F0--F4, and disease groups were ordered control = 0, NAFL = 1 and NASH = 2. Broad NAFLD, obese-only and unclassified labels were excluded from the disease-group analysis. Original diagnoses were not assumed equivalent to contemporary MASLD/MASH criteria \cite{ref3}.

GSE130970 lacked recovered individual author-assigned diagnoses. Operational classifications used histological components: zero steatosis defined a control proxy; positive steatosis with positive ballooning defined a NASH proxy; and positive steatosis with zero ballooning defined a NAFL proxy \cite{ref4}. Five ambiguous classifications were set to unclassified, retaining their fibrosis stages; 73 histology-derived labels remained. These proxies do not constitute complete histopathological diagnoses.

For repeated biopsies, standardised expression was averaged when all recorded stages were known and identical; otherwise, only the first biopsy was retained. Selection preceded stage-specific filtering and was applied throughout discovery, clustering, comparisons and resampling. In GSE193066, 28 same-stage pairs were averaged and 30 different-stage pairs contributed their first biopsy, resolving 164 biopsies to 106 patient observations \cite{ref10}. Studies required at least 25 patient observations and variation on the clinical axis. The fibrosis analysis included 610 observations across five studies; the disease-group analysis included 45 from GSE126848 (excluding 12 obese-only samples), 73 from GSE130970 and 98 from GSE167523 (216 total; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S2}).

\subsection*{Gene harmonisation and expression preprocessing}
Missing and non-numeric measurements were removed, measured zeros were retained, and duplicate raw identifiers were resolved by keeping their first occurrence. The HGNC complete set mapped approved symbols, Entrez identifiers and unambiguous aliases or previous symbols to Ensembl identifiers. Ensembl version suffixes and \texttt{\_PAR\_Y} rows were removed; Ensembl identifiers without HGNC annotation were retained, and mapping failures were recorded. Distinct raw identifiers mapping to the same sample--gene pair were summed.

GSE130970 was transformed as $\log_2(\mathrm{TPM}+1)$; count datasets used $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$, with library totals calculated before mapping and filtering. GSE193066 retained its deposited linear RLE-normalised counts and used $\log_2(\mathrm{count}+1)$ without CPM rescaling \cite{ref9}. No DESeq2 differential-expression model was fitted. Mapped-gene summation preceded transformation, followed by within-study gene standardisation (sample SD, $\mathrm{ddof}=1$), then patient-level selection or averaging without restandardisation. Undefined SDs yielded zero standardised expression. GSE193066 standardisation included all 164 biopsies before patient aggregation. No fitted cross-study batch correction was applied. Effects are in within-study SD units, not fold changes. The graph retained the measured-gene union; association eligibility used the 14,794 genes measured in all eight studies. Dataset-specific processing is detailed in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S2}.

\subsection*{Cross-study association analysis}
Within each eligible study, Spearman correlation related patient-level standardised expression to each ordered clinical axis, using average ranks for ties. Two-sided p-values used $t=\rho\sqrt{(n-2)/(1-\rho^2)}$ with $n-2$ degrees of freedom; correlations were clipped to $\pm0.9999999$. Testability required complete expression coverage and nonconstant ranked expression. Genes required at least three testable studies. The median correlation summarised direction and magnitude; Fisher's statistic combined two-sided p-values independently of direction:
\[
Q_g=-2\sum_{d=1}^{D_g}\log(p_{gd}),\qquad Q_g\sim\chi^2_{2D_g}.
\]
Here, $D_g$ denotes the number of testable studies, and p-values were bounded below by $10^{-300}$.

BH adjustment \cite{ref11} preceded directional filtering and was applied separately to 14,794 fibrosis tests and 14,784 disease-group tests. Ten common genes were constant among the 45 eligible GSE126848 observations and failed the three-study requirement. Selection required $q<0.05$ and every testable study correlation to share the median's positive/non-positive class; no minimum correlation magnitude was imposed. Set membership defined fibrosis-only, disease-group-only and shared genes, with shared genes separated by direction agreement. These categories describe selection in different populations rather than biological specificity. Exported stage means and median correlations were rounded to three decimal places for downstream summaries and scoring.

\subsection*{Fibrosis-stage expression profiles and clustering}
Mean patient-level expression at each stage formed a five-value profile for each selected fibrosis-associated gene. Profiles were row-standardised ($\mathrm{ddof}=0$) and clustered by K-means (50 initialisations; seed 42), emphasising shape. Candidate $k=3$--8 solutions were evaluated by silhouette scores and ARI stability across 50 refits using 80\% gene subsets and a separate 50 within-study patient bootstraps (10 initialisations per diagnostic fit). The largest $k$ with mean gene-subset ARI $\geq0.95$ was selected. Patient-bootstrap stability was assessed separately (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S1}).

A further 100 patient bootstraps assessed membership confidence. Hungarian assignment matched bootstrap and reference centroids by correlation. A gene was assessable only when its reference cluster's matched centroid had $r\geq0.5$; confidence was the fraction of assessable refits returning it to that cluster, with denominators retained. Confidence-core membership required $\geq0.8$. As a sensitivity check, profiles estimated by ordinary least squares with categorical stage and study terms were row-standardised, reclustered and compared by ARI and per-gene correlations. Cluster names describe cross-sectional patterns, not individual longitudinal trajectories.

\subsection*{Adjacent-stage comparisons and matched refits}
F0--F1, F1--F2, F2--F3 and F3--F4 comparisons used selected fibrosis-associated genes and studies represented at both stages. Ordinary least squares estimated the higher-stage effect $\beta_g$ with study-specific intercepts:
\[
z_{ig}=\alpha_g+\beta_g H_i+\sum_{d=2}^{D}\gamma_{gd}I(\mathrm{study}_i=d)+\varepsilon_{ig}.
\]
Two-sided t tests assessed $\beta_g$, with BH correction across the selected fibrosis genes within each contrast. Substantial differences required $q<0.05$ and $|\beta_g|\geq0.2$ SD units.

To assess unequal stage sizes, 50 matched draws (seed 42) sampled without replacement within study--stage pools: two patients per stage from GSE130970, 14 from GSE135251, eight from GSE162694 and six from GSE240729 (30 per stage). Reproducibility was the fraction of draws with $|\beta|\geq0.2$ and the full-data coefficient's sign; significance was recorded separately. Analyses remained conditional on fibrosis-gene selection, and study adjustment did not comprehensively control clinical or cell-composition differences.

\subsection*{Functional enrichment analysis}
GSEApy \cite{ref13} performed one-sided hypergeometric over-representation tests using MSigDB 2024.1.Hs GO Biological Process, KEGG legacy, Reactome and Hallmark collections \cite{ref12}. The background contained 14,794 unique harmonised symbols corresponding one-to-one to the common Ensembl eligibility universe. Annotation sets were intersected with this background and restricted to 10--500 genes; input groups required at least five genes. BH adjustment was applied within each group and collection ($q<0.05$).

Tests covered increasing and decreasing fibrosis-only, disease-group-only and concordant shared groups, five full clusters and five confidence-core sets. Discordant shared genes were excluded from the shared direction groups. Outputs included overlap genes, counts, fold enrichment and adjusted p-values; full-cluster/core comparisons assessed membership sensitivity. Undersized groups were marked untested. Enrichment describes annotation over-representation rather than demonstrated pathway activation.

\subsection*{Gene prioritisation and robustness assessment}
Fibrosis-associated genes received the nine-point score
\[
S_g=P_g+T_g+X_g+F_g+B_g.
\]
$P_g$ scored median association magnitude (0--2); $T_g$, direction-consistent F0--F4 separation (0--2); $X_g$, the strongest adjacent-stage contrast and matched-refit reproducibility (0--2); $F_g$, operational fibrosis-only membership (0--1); and $B_g$, support from enriched annotation collections (0--2). Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S3} specifies every threshold, rounding rule, tie-break and missing-evidence rule. Missing selected-contrast evidence stopped scoring rather than assigning zero. Annotation support used all significant terms from the gene's concordant direction group or full cluster, counted each collection once and excluded confidence-core enrichment. Tier 1 required $S_g\geq7$, Tier 2 required $5\leq S_g<7$, and Tier 3 comprised the remainder. Up to five Tier 1 genes per cluster were selected by descending score, then absolute median fibrosis correlation. Correlated score components provide prioritisation rather than independent validation.

LOSO analysis omitted each fibrosis study in turn, repeated patient-level associations and applied BH correction across the 14,794-gene family. Tier 1 summaries separately assessed $q<0.05$, direction and $|\operatorname{median}\rho|\geq0.30$. This assessed internal robustness, not independent held-out prediction.

Sensitivity analyses compared a baseline retaining all 78 original derived GSE130970 classifications and CPM-rescaling GSE193066; labels-only exclusion of the five ambiguous classifications; preservation of deposited GSE193066 RLE normalisation alone; and both changes together. The combined configuration was primary: the five cases were unclassified only for disease-group analysis. Biopsy handling, eligibility, $k=5$, score thresholds, cluster quotas and annotation selection were fixed. Further checks varied score thresholds, removed individual components and used an alternative cluster-top-score shortlist. The $F_g$ ablation retained the Tier 1 threshold of seven while reducing the maximum score to eight.

\subsection*{Knowledge-graph construction and analysis}
Neo4j integrated expression measurements with OptimusKG using Ensembl identifiers \cite{ref5}. Drug annotations from ChEMBL, DGIdb, GtoPdb and Open Targets enriched the resource but did not contribute to the reported transcriptomic or prioritisation analyses. Eight node categories represented Gene, Sample, Dataset, Disease, Drug, Pathway, BioProcess and Phenotype. \texttt{EXPRESSES} stored raw and standardised sample--gene measurements; \texttt{IN\_DATASET} linked samples to studies. Sample properties retained patient identity and biopsy order.

Curated relations represented gene associations and interactions, pathway/process membership, disease hierarchies, phenotypes and drug actions or indications. Open Targets gene--disease scores supplied through OptimusKG required $\mathrm{score}\geq0.1$; symmetric interactions were deduplicated. Drug action, indication, contraindication and off-label meanings were retained. \texttt{TRACKS\_FIBROSIS} and \texttt{TRACKS\_INFLAMMATION} stored selected associations and their statistics; the latter denotes ordered disease-group associations, not direct inflammation measurement. Graph composition, the 14 relationship types and property coverage are described in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S4} and the schema. Association records and displayed subgraph connections were checked against analysis exports.

For a focused annotation network, hypergeometric tests assessed Tier 1 over-representation using the common 14,794-Ensembl-gene background, equivalent to the functional-enrichment symbol background. Annotation sizes counted background-intersecting members; unannotated genes remained in the denominator. BH correction covered all 3,980 Pathway/BioProcess nodes reached by Tier 1 genes before display filtering. Eligible annotations required 10--500 background genes, at least ten Tier 1 members and $q<0.05$. Ranked by adjusted p-value, annotations were skipped if their Tier 1 member sets had Jaccard similarity $>0.5$ to a retained set; twelve were retained. Display included genes linked to at least three selected annotations or shortlisted genes linked to at least one. This contextual network was not independent validation.

\subsection*{Software and reproducibility}
Analyses used Python 3.12.14, pandas 2.3.3, NumPy 2.2.4, SciPy 1.16.3, scikit-learn 1.7.2, matplotlib 3.10.7 and GSEApy 1.3.1; the graph used Neo4j Enterprise 2026.09.0. Clustering and matched refits used seed 42. Code, runtime requirements, tables and provenance manifests document the procedures. The expression-analysis input contains the common 14,794-gene core rather than the complete graph expression layer.

The OptimusKG deposit is identified by DOI 10.7910/DVN/IYNGEV. Formal HGNC/OptimusKG release identifiers and expression-data acquisition dates were not recorded. Retained reference-file modification dates and SHA-256 checksums are documented in the Supplementary Methods and source manifest; these do not establish official release dates. No new human participant data were collected.

\section*{Conclusion}
Liver fibrosis severity was associated with heterogeneous expression patterns that can be organised alongside cross-study consistency, stage contrasts and curated biological annotations. The practical value of this framework is that candidate genes can be examined together with the evidence supporting their ranking and the uncertainty surrounding their stage-pattern assignment. Association strength, cluster confidence and graph connectivity contribute different forms of information and should remain distinguishable when the resource is used for further research. The present evidence supports gene prioritisation and biological-context assessment, rather than validated patient prediction or causal conclusions.

Three priorities follow from these boundaries. First, independent cohorts with harmonised clinical labels and longitudinal sampling should test the reproducibility and temporal interpretation of the candidate associations, using nested selection where performance is evaluated. Second, cell-resolved or spatial measurements should determine whether the bulk-liver patterns reflect cellular composition, cell-state changes or both, followed by targeted functional experiments. Third, an immutable public release of the analysis code, graph exports, source manifests and complete supplementary outputs should support independent reproduction and fair comparisons under shared evaluation conditions. These steps would establish the scope in which the resource and its prioritised genes can be used reliably.

Define A: $q<0.05$ and $|\beta|\geq0.20$ in that comparison. Define B: at least 50\% of matched draws have $|\beta|\geq0.20$ and the same sign as the selected full-data coefficient. Assign \textbf{2} if A and B hold, \textbf{1} if exactly one holds, and \textbf{0} if neither holds. Matched-draw significance does not contribute. \\
$F_g$ & Operational fibrosis-only membership & \textbf{1}: selected in the fibrosis analysis but not in the ordered disease-group analysis. \textbf{0}: otherwise. This selection status does not establish biological specificity. \\
$B_g$ & Functional-annotation support & Count annotation collections containing at least one significantly enriched term (BH $q<0.05$) whose overlapping-gene list contains the gene, using its own concordant direction group or its full expression cluster. \textbf{2}: at least two collections; \textbf{1}: one; \textbf{0}: none. Eligible collections: GO Biological Process, KEGG legacy, Reactome and Hallmark. Each counts once across both routes; confidence-core enrichment is excluded. \\
\end{longtable}
\small
\textbf{Precision and thresholds.} Scoring uses exported median correlations and stage means recorded to three decimal places; the endpoint difference is also rounded to three decimals before comparison. Lower score thresholds are inclusive; statistical significance requires $q<0.05$.

\textbf{Contrast selection and missing evidence.} Exact ties in $|\beta|$ retain the first comparison in the order F0--F1, F1--F2, F2--F3, F3--F4. A missing full-data q-value does not satisfy condition A. Missing matched evidence for the selected contrast, or absence of a selected contrast, stops scoring rather than automatically assigning zero. All 3,845 fibrosis-associated genes had finite full-data coefficients and q-values for all four contrasts and matched evidence for the selected contrast.

\textbf{Annotation eligibility.} All significant terms are eligible, including terms not displayed in figures. Discordant shared genes have no concordant direction-group route but can receive support through their full expression cluster. Multiple terms within one collection do not increase its contribution.

\textbf{Total score and shortlist.} $S_g=P_g+T_g+X_g+F_g+B_g$ has a maximum of nine. Tier 1: $S_g\geq7$; Tier 2: $5\leq S_g<7$; Tier 3: $S_g<5$. Up to five Tier 1 genes per cluster are selected by descending total score, then descending absolute median fibrosis correlation. Clusters with fewer than five qualifying genes contribute fewer candidates. The components share evidence and do not constitute independent validation.

\subsection*{Supplementary Methods: implementation checks and reference-file inventory}
\paragraph{Ambiguous disease classifications.} The five GSE130970 samples assigned unclassified disease-group status in the primary analysis were GSM3758028, GSM3758038, GSM3758039, GSM3758073 and GSM3758074. Their original histological fields and fibrosis stages were retained.

\paragraph{Relationship schema.} The integrated graph used 14 relationship types: \texttt{EXPRESSES}, \texttt{IN\_DATASET}, \texttt{ASSOCIATED\_WITH}, \texttt{INTERACTS\_WITH}, \texttt{INVOLVED\_IN}, \texttt{IN\_PATHWAY}, \texttt{ACTS\_ON}, \texttt{TREATS}, \texttt{CONTRAINDICATED\_IN}, \texttt{OFF\_LABEL\_FOR}, \texttt{HAS\_PHENOTYPE}, \texttt{PARENT\_OF}, \texttt{TRACKS\_FIBROSIS} and \texttt{TRACKS\_INFLAMMATION}.

For transition scoring, the comparison with the largest absolute available full-data coefficient was selected, with ties resolved in the order F0--F1, F1--F2, F2--F3, F3--F4. A missing full-data q-value did not satisfy the substantial-difference condition. Missing matched evidence for the selected comparison, or the absence of a selected comparison for a gene, stopped scoring through an assertion rather than being assigned zero. In the final analysis, all 3,845 fibrosis-associated genes had finite coefficients and q-values for all four comparisons and matched evidence for the selected comparison. The reconstructed P/T/X/F/B scores agreed with the analysis table for all 3,845 genes.

The reference files read by the analysis scripts were \nolinkurl{data/raw/hgnc_complete_set.txt} (file modification date 20 August 2026; most recent gene-record modification date within the file 18 August 2026) and the OptimusKG parquet files in \nolinkurl{data/optimuskg} (file modification date 1 September 2026). SHA-256 checksums for all files are provided in the source manifest, \nolinkurl{source_checksums.txt}. File dates and checksums identify these retained files and do not establish official release dates.
