## What the source does and does not contain (honest notes)

- **Present on every Neo4j edge:** consolidated action, per-source assertions (`relation_assertions`), contributing sources (`sources_direct`: DrugBank, Open Targets, etc.), number of sources, evidence level (single-/multi-source), conflict flag.
- **Present only on some edges:** mechanism-of-action text, literature URLs (PubMed/Europe PMC), indirect sources.
- **Not available:** a per-source record or accession ID for each assertion. `Database_ID` is therefore the stable pair `ChEMBL drug ID | Ensembl gene ID`. There is also no quantitative affinity/potency, and no dose or tissue context.
- The CSV export (`edges_drug_gene.csv`) keeps only drug, gene and action; its `score` column is empty for drug–gene edges. The extra evidence fields come from Neo4j only.
- Action labels such as TARGET, ENZYME, TRANSPORTER and CARRIER describe what the gene product is for the drug, not whether the drug switches it down or up. They are counted as **unknown direction**.

## Why degree matters (study bias)
Background genes with more known protein–protein interactions are more likely to have a known drug:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 571 | 0–0 | 6.8 | 5.8 |
| 1 | 1423 | 1–3 | 11.2 | 8.6 |
| 2 | 1422 | 3–6 | 12.1 | 9.5 |
| 3 | 1422 | 6–10 | 14.6 | 12.7 |
| 4 | 1422 | 10–15 | 14.3 | 12.5 |
| 5 | 1423 | 15–21 | 13.9 | 12.5 |
| 6 | 1422 | 21–28 | 13.3 | 11.8 |
| 7 | 1422 | 28–38 | 14.8 | 13.4 |
| 8 | 1422 | 38–53 | 14.1 | 13.2 |
| 9 | 1422 | 53–88 | 15.5 | 14.8 |
| 10 | 1423 | 88–5189 | 24.4 | 23.8 |

A raw difference can therefore just mean "better-studied genes". The degree-matched permutation removes this: a group counts as more or less druggable **beyond connectivity** only if its observed % lies outside its degree-matched null.

## Interpretation and caveats
- Percentages describe **representation among known drug targets in the knowledge graph**. They partly reflect which genes scientists have studied and drugged (DrugBank/Open Targets annotation bias), not biology alone.
- Druggability is not therapeutic relevance. A drug-connected gene is a *repurposing hypothesis entry point*, not evidence a drug would help.
- Groups are kept separate as in Paper 1. The 30 shared genes with discordant direction are reported on their own and are small (wide CIs).

===== task5_prep_pathway_map (exit 0, 28s)

**Scope:** knowledge graph + Paper 1 only (project scope decision); no external pathway resources.
**Graph source:** Neo4j (live liver_kg, read-only). Graph members: 46,751 Gene→Pathway (Reactome) and 157,081 Gene→BioProcess (GO BP) lines.

## The problem
Paper 1 enrichment used four collections (GO BP, Reactome, KEGG, Hallmark). The graph holds only GO BP (`BioProcess`) and Reactome (`Pathway`). KEGG and Hallmark terms have no graph node of their own.

## The fix (two matching steps, best first)
1. **name**: identical normalised name, same collection (GO→BioProcess, Reactome→Pathway).
2. **gene_overlap_proxy**: the graph node whose progression-gene members best overlap the term's Paper 1 genes (Jaccard ≥ 0.5, ≥ 5 shared genes). This is how KEGG/Hallmark terms reach the graph. Proxies are labelled and must be reported as approximate.

Fuzzy (character-similarity) name matching was tried and **rejected**: it paired different biology (e.g. "catabolic" with "metabolic", "type II" with "type III" interferon, "amide" with "amine").

Terms that stay unlinked are still usable: `paper1_term_genes_long.csv` holds their Paper 1 gene lists, so MetaPath M7 can test Drug → Gene ∈ term directly.

## Limitations
1. **88.5% of drug–gene links have unknown action direction** (TARGET/ENZYME/TRANSPORTER/CARRIER). Direction-consistency can only be judged for 1,001 links, and unknown is not the same as inconsistent.
2. **No affinity or potency values** in the graph, so drug strength cannot be weighted.
3. **No per-assertion accession IDs.** Links are identified by ChEMBL drug ID | Ensembl gene ID with source names and, where available, literature URLs.
4. **Knowledge-graph study bias:** well-studied (high-degree) genes have more known drugs (background druggability rises from 5.8% to 23.8% across degree bins). This is addressed with degree-matched nulls and degree-normalised MetaPath support, but not removed.
5. **Graph housekeeping (does not affect results):** in the live Neo4j graph the `fibrosis_rho` property stored on Gene nodes is from the pre-correction Paper 1 run (3,645 genes; missing for 391 current fibrosis genes). The `TRACKS_FIBROSIS` edges are current and match the corrected Paper 1 table exactly. Paper 2 uses only the corrected tables and edges, but the node property should be refreshed by the Paper 1 team.
6. "No liver indication" means none is recorded in the graph, not necessarily none in practice. EFO filed some non-hepatic diseases under "liver disease"; the cleaning rule is documented.
7. Target-score weights were frozen as equal weights for the final ranking (`Final_ranking/PREREGISTRATION.md`); Task 6 keeps the design test that motivated the two-list, stable-target reporting.
8. Scope limits by design: no transcriptomic reversal, no external validation.
